The case for unfinished notebooks

Why the pages you abandon teach you more than the ones you finish.

EO Elena Ortiz · March 19, 2026 · 9 min read

I have eleven notebooks on the shelf above my desk, and not one of them is finished. For years that felt like a quiet failure — proof that I start more than I complete. Then I started reading them.

The abandoned pages are where the thinking is. The finished ones mostly hold decisions; the half-written ones hold the arguments that led there, the wrong turns, the sketches of things I almost built.

Keep the mess where you can find it

A working notebook isn’t an archive. It’s a scratchpad with a memory. Three habits made mine useful again:

  1. Date every page. Not for history — for search. “Sometime in March” is enough to find a thought.
  2. Leave the first page blank. When the notebook is retired, it becomes the index of what mattered.
  3. Never tear anything out. The page you’d remove is usually the one you’ll want in a year.

Finishing is overrated

We treat a full notebook like a finished book. But a notebook isn’t a product; it’s a place. You don’t finish a workshop, you work in it. When you stop expecting the last page, the first one gets braver.

So start a new one tomorrow, even if the old one has room. Some thoughts only arrive on a clean page.
